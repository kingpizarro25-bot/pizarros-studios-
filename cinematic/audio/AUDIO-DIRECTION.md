# Sound direction

The sound does half the storytelling. The picture shows disorder becoming order; the sound
should make you *feel* it, mostly by turning noise into rhythm.

**Do not use the AI-generated audio that comes with the video clips.** Turn it off at
generation time. It will not know the plan and it will fight the design.

## The idea in one line

> Random noises slowly become a rhythm, and then the rhythm resolves and stops.

## Act by act

**Act 1 — Fragmentation (0:00–0:09.5)**
Dry, sparse, uncomfortable. Sounds happen with no relationship to each other: a sheet of paper
shifting, distant air conditioning, a single phone notification. Real silence in the gaps.
Nothing musical. The audience should feel slightly unsettled without knowing why.

**Act 2 — Connection (0:09.5–0:19.5)**
A low pulse enters, quiet, around 90 beats per minute. It does nothing but keep time. Then the
sound effects begin landing *on* that pulse instead of randomly — the clicks in Shot 5 and Shot 6
are the first sounds in the film to arrive on the beat. That is the entire trick, and it works
even on people who never notice it.

**Act 3 — Intelligence (0:19.5–0:31.5)**
The pulse becomes a bed. Layers lock together. A low tone starts rising underneath. At Shot 9 —
the human decision — **everything drops away for a moment** and there is a single quiet click.
Silence is the loudest tool available; spend it here.

**Act 4 — Control (0:31.5–0:40)**
The tension resolves into one clean chord and then falls away to almost nothing. The name appears
in near-silence. Never end on a big hit.

## The sound library

| Sound | Where it is used | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Paper shift | Shots 1, 5 | Dry, close, real |
| Room tone | Throughout | Barely audible, gives the space a size |
| Notification | Shot 3 | Cold, uneven, three of them |
| Connection click | Shots 4, 5, 6 | Very short, dry, like a latch |
| Low pulse | Acts 2–3 | Felt more than heard |
| Confirmation tone | Shot 7 | Clean, single, slightly warm |
| Rising low tone | Act 3 | Long, under everything |
| Resolving chord | Shot 11 | Warm, unhurried, one only |

## Banned

Whooshes. Risers. The big brass hit from movie trailers. Typing sounds. Sci-fi computer beeps.
Anything that sounds like a stock "technology" music bed. Voiceover.

## Music

If you use a track at all, it should do less than you think — a low pulse and one sustained tone
is genuinely enough for forty seconds. The temptation will be to add a track that "builds". A
track that builds will drag the film toward every other AI company video ever made.
