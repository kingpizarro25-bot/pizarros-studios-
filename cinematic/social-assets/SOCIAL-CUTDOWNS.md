# Short versions for social media

One film, cut several ways. Shoot once, publish many times.

## The cuts

| Version | Shape | Length | Shots used | For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full film** | Wide | 40s | 1–12 | The website, a pitch, YouTube |
| **The short** | Tall | 15s | 1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12 | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts |
| **The hook** | Tall | 6s | 4, 9, 12 | Adverts, stories |
| **Silent loop** | Wide | 3s | 4 alone | Behind the website heading |
| **One frame** | Square | still | Shot 10 or 8 | Profile posts, link previews |

## Making the tall versions

Do not just crop the wide version — the composition falls apart. Two options, in order of
preference:

1. **Generate the shot again** at `9:16` using the same prompt. Costs credits, looks right.
2. **Rebuild the frame** in the editor: put the wide clip in the middle of a tall black canvas
   and let the black space carry the text. This suits our style unusually well, because the
   film is mostly black anyway.

## Rules that still apply

- Every clip must work **with the sound off**. Most people will never hear it.
- The name appears at the end, not the start. Front-loading a logo makes it look like an advert
  and people scroll past.
- No captions on the brand film. If a clip needs explaining, it is the wrong clip.
- The first second has to earn the next second. Shot 4 — the cyan line appearing — is the
  strongest opener you have. That is why the short versions lead with it rather than with the
  scattered paper.
