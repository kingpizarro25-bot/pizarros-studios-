# Reusable asset library

The point of this folder is that the second film costs far less effort than the first. Every
approved frame, sound, and setting becomes raw material for the next one.

## What to keep, and why

### Settings
The same office, reused forever. Keep the approved wide shots of it — feed them in as reference
pictures so future films happen in the same building.
- The open-plan room before dawn (Shot 2)
- The same room at dawn (Shot 11)
- The desk, close (Shot 1)
- The glass partition corner (Shot 7)

### Ideas expressed as pictures
Abstract shots that mean "intelligence" without a robot in sight.
- The cyan line travelling along a real edge (Shot 4) — **the single most reusable asset**
- Documents settling into a grid (Shot 5)
- Light lines following the building's own lines (Shot 8)

### Screens
Restrained, believable interface shots. Build a small set showing: checking, connecting,
confirming, analysing. Never use a real customer's data.

### Objects and materials
- The machined aluminium block (Shot 10) — this is the studio's hero object, reuse it
- Paper stock, dark glass, braided cable, brushed metal, concrete

### Backgrounds
Pure black, graphite gradient, dark glass with faint reflection. Useful behind titles and behind
anything on the website.

### Transitions
Keep every clean cut point. Also keep any frame that ends on near-black — those are free
transition points between shots.

### The name
The title card, in three forms: on black, on a dark photograph, and as a still image for social.

### Sound
Build a small library and reuse it: the dry click, the low pulse, the paper shift, the
confirmation tone, room tone, the final chord.

### Recurring motifs — the things that make it recognisable
1. **The line** — a thin cyan line travelling along a real surface
2. **Settling** — objects arriving into order, never snapping
3. **Coming into focus** — the film literally sharpens as it resolves
4. **One warm point** in a cold frame, meaning a person is there
5. **Two colours, two meanings** — cyan is the system, amber is the human

## Reference pictures worth collecting from outside

You do not need to buy anything, but it helps to have a few real photographs pinned up for
comparison. Look for:
- Architectural photography at night, where most of the frame is black
- Watch and camera product photography, for how metal is lit
- Documentary photography of control rooms and workshops, for believable screens
- Any film that trusts darkness rather than filling every shadow

The test when comparing: does our frame look like a photograph someone took, or like something
a computer made? Photograph wins every time.
